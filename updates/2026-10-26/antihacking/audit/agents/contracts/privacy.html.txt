1. Your project stays on your device Project answers, prompts, plans, attachments and ZIP generation stay on your device. The account and Blog services do not receive project contents or selected files. No project analytics, advertising trackers, hosted AI, cloud project sync or sale of project data is enabled. Draft answers, options and position are saved in this browser using an immediate local journal and IndexedDB, bound to the verified account and session. Only the attachment count is retained, without file names or bytes. Selected files and the prepared ZIP remain in tab memory. Project content is not synchronized with the account service. This draft is not a backup; it may be lost when site data is cleared or the browser removes its storage. The localStorage journal key is accreatio:account-draft:v1 ; IndexedDB uses database accreatio-drafts , store drafts , key account-current:v1 . Both contain the same draft data categories, including project and planned-participant names, options, position, language and attachment count, without selected file names or bytes. Saving can fail; follow the displayed status. Files must be selected again after reload. Browser settings, private browsing and storage removal can erase the draft; another browser, device or origin has separate storage. There is no project copy on our servers to recover. The context marker accreatio:draft-context:v1 is stored in same-origin localStorage and the same IndexedDB store. It holds opaque account/session labels and a generation value, not sign-in credentials. Clearing the account draft replaces it with a generation-only deletion marker, without account/session labels or project contents, until the next account binding or browser site-data removal. It has no automatic expiry. For a full-page OAuth return, sessionStorage accreatio:draft-oauth-handoff:v1 may temporarily hold only opaque ownerKey / sessionKey labels and expiresAt , without project contents or credentials. It is created only after saving a draft from a known session and before the OAuth redirect. It is valid for at most ten minutes and one use, consumed only after a successful return and verification of the same current owner. Expired, mismatched or malformed metadata and unconfirmed reads/removal cannot restore a draft. Sign out or account change removes it; every new sign-in must remove and confirm the absence of the previous marker before creating another, even without a loaded workspace. Expiry is checked on read, not a promise of timed physical browser deletion. This allows same-owner reauthentication to retain the saved draft without restoring one after explicit sign out; it grants no account access and adds no project collection. Local storage accreatio:onboarding remembers completion of the introduction with a schema version and completion flag. It contains no account or project contents and has no automatic expiry. A service worker caches public application and legal assets in Cache Storage; authentication and account API requests use the network and are excluded from this public cache. A prepared ZIP and its same-operation retry information may remain in page memory until replaced, cleared, New draft or reload. During temporary suspension, unavailable/offline session verification or reauthentication, the builder stays locked and clears its prepared ZIP and retry information from memory. The saved draft stays hidden and can resume only after the same owner is verified again. Explicit sign out, account deletion or an account change discards that draft. This is not a server backup. New draft clears the current project draft and prepared-ZIP recovery; it does not revoke your account session or remove downloaded files, onboarding preferences or all public caches. Signing out, deleting or changing an account clears the current site draft, selected-file references and prepared ZIP. A later sign-in starts a new blank draft. Previously downloaded files remain under your control. Cookie preferences, introduction completion and account allowance are independent; clearing a draft or signing out does not reset that allowance. Use browser site-data controls for remaining local storage and manage downloaded files separately. 4. Allowance, retention and deletion The service authorizes five new exports per account in a rolling 24 hours, shared across sessions/devices. Same-operation retry within 48 hours does not consume another slot. Receipts contain operation identifiers and dates, not project names, contents or ZIP bytes. Editing, dry review and cancellation do not consume allowance. Displayed availability does not prove a physical file was saved. Active account data is kept while the account is maintained, until deletion or a handled removal request; no automatic inactivity-deletion deadline is promised. Deletion clears active contact, display name, linked identities and the authorization receipt and revokes sessions. Protected deletion locators, minimum allowance receipts and minimal account-abuse state remain for 48 hours to prevent quota reset; they do not keep deleted email or provider subject in plaintext. The technical abuse guard stores HMAC-derived network, opaque-account and hashed-session keys with fixed route classes, counters, cooldowns and expiry in the separate account database. A network key represents one IPv4 address or an IPv6 /64 prefix. These are protected, linkable security data, not anonymous analytics. The guard does not store raw IP addresses, email addresses, user agents, project content or request bodies. Network and session pacing alone does not suspend an account. Only repeated eligible excess attributable to a valid ordinary account can trigger the temporary suspensions described in the Terms; no content/AI decision, automatic permanent ban or shared-IP/email-coincidence ban is enabled. Request-counter buckets expire one hour after the last admitted request or newly imposed cooldown; blocked retries do not extend that deadline. Capacity is capped at 8,192 buckets and 4,096 account-abuse states, with reserved capacity for rights requests. Minimal account-abuse state stores an opaque account ID, episode counts, temporary restriction level and dates for a 48-hour recurrence window. Account deletion clears active personal/account data but does not reset this minimal abuse state early; it remains until that window expires or physical account-row cleanup occurs first. Expired security rows are removed in bounded maintenance and request-time cleanup; expiry prevents continued use but is not a promise of exact physical deletion time. No permanent banned-person ledger is created. OAuth secrets expire in ten minutes and are cleared on consumption/cancellation; protected admission metadata has a one-hour window. Sessions last up to 24 hours. Eligible 48-hour security/allowance/deletion records and expired material are removed by bounded scheduled maintenance on its next successful run. A configured hourly schedule is not a guarantee of exact physical deletion; failures or backlog can delay it and require operator action. Own email OTP remains disabled in the current production configuration. If separately enabled later, its requested transport processes recipient, code and expiry; the account service keeps a keyed verifier, not a plaintext code, with ten-minute validity, five attempts and one use. Minimal abuse metadata has a 48-hour window. Enabling that method requires an accurate updated operational record. Application cleanup periods do not establish provider log, recovery-history, mailbox or backup retention. Those periods and deletion capabilities require their own confirmed configuration. No unverified provider-retention duration or country-exclusive storage is promised.
